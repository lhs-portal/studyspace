4.7.4 Consequences of inflation

  • Savers: Lose purchasing power if nominal interest rates on savings accounts are lower than the inflation rate.
  • Lenders: Lose money because the real value of future debt repayments is reduced by inflation.
  • Borrowers: Benefit because they repay loans in the future using money that has less purchasing power than when originally borrowed.
  • Consumers: Experience a fall in real purchasing power unless wages increase at or above the rate of inflation.
  • Workers: May demand higher wages to keep up with living costs, potentially triggering a wage-price spiral.
  • Producers/Firms: Face menu costs (updating price lists) and shoe-leather costs, while heightened uncertainty hampers long-term investment planning.
  • The economy suffers reduced international competitiveness as exports become relatively more expensive, potentially worsening the current account balance.
