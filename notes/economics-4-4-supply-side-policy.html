4.4.1 Definition of supply-side policy

  • Supply-side policy refers to government measures designed to increase aggregate supply, expand productive capacity, and boost potential economic growth.

4.4.2 Supply-side policy measures

  • Education and training: Improving human capital and worker skill levels to enhance labour productivity.
  • Infrastructure spending: Upgrading transport, communication, and energy networks to reduce transport costs and efficiency bottlenecks.
  • Labour market reforms: Reducing trade union power or adjusting unemployment benefits to increase labor market flexibility.
  • Lower direct taxes: Reducing income tax to increase work incentives and cutting corporation tax to encourage investment.
  • Deregulation: Removing unnecessary red tape and regulatory burdens to foster business competition and lower compliance costs.
  • Improving incentives to work and invest: Offering tax credits or investment allowances to stimulate innovation and capital stock additions.
  • Privatisation: Transferring state-owned assets to the private sector to increase efficiency through profit motivation.

4.4.3 Effects of supply-side policy measures on government macroeconomic aims

  • Economic growth & Employment: Increases potential GDP output and creates sustainable long-term job opportunities without generating inflationary pressure.
  • Price stability & Trade: Helps reduce cost-push inflation and enhances international export competitiveness by raising domestic productivity.
