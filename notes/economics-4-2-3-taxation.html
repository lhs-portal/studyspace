Economics ? Government and the Macroeconomy

4.2.3 Taxation

  • Reasons for taxation: raising revenue for government spending, discouraging the consumption of demerit goods, reducing imports to protect domestic industries, redistributing income to lower inequality, influencing total macroeconomic demand, and encouraging environmental sustainability.
  • Classifications of tax:
  • Progressive tax: Takes a higher percentage of income as income increases (e.g., income tax).
  • Regressive tax: Takes a lower percentage of income as income increases (e.g., sales tax or VAT).
  • Proportional tax: Takes the same percentage of income across all income levels.
  • Direct tax: Levied directly on income or wealth (e.g., personal income tax, corporation tax).
  • Indirect tax: levied on goods and services, paid via suppliers (e.g., GST/VAT, excise duties).
  • Impact of taxation: Affects disposable income and spending power for consumers, work incentives for workers, production costs and profit margins for producers/firms, total revenue collected by the government, and total demand and resource allocation in the economy.
