4.3.1 Definitions of money supply and monetary policy

  • Money supply: The total amount of monetary assets available in an economy at a specific time, including notes, coins, and bank deposits.
  • Monetary policy: Decisions made by the central bank or government regarding money supply, interest rates, and exchange rates to influence total demand and achieve macroeconomic aims.

4.3.2 Monetary policy measures

  • Interest rates: the cost of borrowing and reward for saving. Raising interest rates reduces borrowing and increases saving, dampening consumer and business expenditure; lowering interest rates encourages borrowing and expenditure.
  • Money supply: Central banks can control liquidity in the economy through quantitative easing or reserve requirements, altering the overall availability of credit.
  • Foreign exchange rate: Interventions in currency markets or changes in interest rates influence national exchange rate values, impacting export and import competitiveness.

4.3.3 Effect of monetary policy on government macroeconomic aims

  • Expansionary monetary policy: Involves lowering interest rates or increasing the money supply to stimulate aggregate demand, boost GDP growth, and lower cyclical unemployment.
  • Contractionary monetary policy: Involves raising interest rates or reducing the money supply to dampen total demand and curb demand-pull inflation to achieve price stability.
