4.5.1 Definition of economic growth

  • Economic growth is an expansion in the output of goods and services produced in an economy over a given period.

The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and Government Revenue Explained in One Minute

4.5.2 Measurement of economic growth

  • Real Gross Domestic Product (GDP): The total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country, adjusted for inflation.

4.5.3 Causes and consequences of economic growth

  • Causes: Driven by increases in total demand, or increases in the quantity and quality of resources (factors of production) available.
  • Advantages: Higher living standards, increased employment, expanded tax revenues for government spending, and reduced poverty levels.
  • Disadvantages: Potential demand-pull inflation, environmental degradation, depletion of natural resources, and widening income inequality if benefits are unevenly distributed.

4.5.4 Causes and consequences of recession

  • Definition of recession: A period of decline in real GDP for two consecutive quarters.
  • Causes: Triggered by severe reductions in total demand, negative supply shocks, or decreases in the quantity and quality of economic resources.
  • Consequences: Loss of employment and income for consumers, declining revenues and bankruptcies for firms, and falling tax revenue alongside higher welfare payments for the government.

4.5.5 Policies to promote economic growth

  • Governments utilize expansionary fiscal policy (tax cuts, public investment), expansionary monetary policy (lower interest rates), and supply-side measures (education, deregulation) to promote sustainable economic growth.
