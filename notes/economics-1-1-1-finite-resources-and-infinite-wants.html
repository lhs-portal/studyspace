Economics ? 1. The Basic Economic Problem

1.1.1 Finite resources and infinite wants

Resources are the inputs used to produce goods and services.

Scarcity: a situation where there are not enough resources to satisfy everyone's wants.

The basic economic problem: where unlimited wants are greater than finite resources. The problem is that there is a scarcity of resources to satisfy all human wants and needs because there are finite resources and unlimited wants.

So, choices have 10 be made about how resources are to be used.

This is applicable to consumers, producers, workers and the government, in how they manage their resources.

1.1.2 Resource Allocation Decisions

All economies have to answer three fundamental economic questions:

  • What to produce?
  • How to produce it?
  • Who is to receive the products produced?

    • The economic questions arise because of the basic economic problem. A decision has to be made as to how the economy's resources are to be allocated. For example, how many resources should be allocated to healthcare, how many to leisure goods and services and how many to educate.

    1.1.3 Economic Goods and Free Goods

    Economic goods are those that require resources to produce it. So, they are limited in supply and have an economic cost and/or consumed with a price. In other words, an economic good is a good with an opportunity cost.

    For example, a carpet is an economic good (see Figure 1.4). The material and labour used to produce it could have been used to make another good (or goods).

    All the goods we buy are economic goods, from bottled water to clothes. Your education is an economic good, since your teachers and the other resources used to provide it could have been employed to make other products.

    Free goods are those which do not require any resources to make it.

    They are abundant in supply, usually referring to natural sources such as air and sunlight.

    Sunshine is one such example, and so is water in a river. However, as soon as this water is processed for drinking or used for irrigation of fields, it becomes an economic good.
